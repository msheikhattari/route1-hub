# Scripture and Study Resources on Human Trafficking
(compiled 2026-10-01 by Claude research agent; verse wording checked vs. BibleGateway/BibleHub ESV & NIV; "listing only"/"unverified" flags preserved)

## PART A — SCRIPTURE

### A1. Narratives of people sold, taken, or exploited
- **Joseph (Gen 37:12-36; 39; 45; 50:20).** Brothers sell him for twenty shekels (37:28); resold in Egypt; sexually harassed by owner's wife; imprisoned on false charge; "you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good" (50:20). The Bible's longest narrative told from a trafficking survivor's POV; FAMILY sold him — matching how most trafficking begins. Angle: does 50:20 excuse the brothers? Hold "God meant it for good" alongside the crime (45:1-2 weeping). TGC essay: https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/finding-hope-in-the-fight-against-human-trafficking/
- **Hagar (Gen 16; 21:8-21).** Egyptian slave given for reproductive use, then driven out. God names her, sees her; she names God "El Roi" (16:13). Angle: God sees and hears but does not immediately free her — how do we sit with partial rescue?
- **Dinah (Gen 34).** Seized and raped; "negotiation" treats her as property; brothers' vengeance condemned (49:5-7). Angle: Dinah never speaks — what does her recorded silence mean?
- **Judah and Tamar (Gen 38).** Denied levirate protection, Tamar poses as a prostitute; Judah buys sex then wants her executed until exposed. Angle: economic desperation as pipeline; the buyer's hypocrisy named. "Survival sex" today?
- **Israel in Egypt / Exodus (Exod 1-14).** Forced labor, infanticide; "I have surely seen the affliction of my people... heard their cry" (3:7); "I will deliver you from slavery... redeem you with an outstretched arm" (6:6). Governing paradigm; "remember that you were a slave in Egypt" (Deut 15:15; 24:18, 22). Angle: an "Exodus people" toward trafficked neighbors?
- **Captive women (Deut 21:10-14; Judg 5:30; Judg 21).** War-captive sex slavery regulated/exposed; Judges 21 mass abduction at Shiloh; "everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (21:25).
- **The Israelite girl in Naaman's house (2 Kgs 5:2-3).** "the Syrians on one of their raids had carried off a little girl from the land of Israel, and she worked in the service of Naaman's wife." Child abduction into domestic servitude in one verse; she points her captor to healing. Angle: don't read her kindness as "redeeming" her captivity.
- **The widow and debt slavery (2 Kgs 4:1-7).** Creditor coming "to take my two children to be his slaves"; Elisha's oil pays the debt. Debt bondage = most common form of trafficking globally. Angle: church's role in debt relief.
- **Nehemiah 5:1-13.** "we are forcing our sons and our daughters to be slaves, and some of our daughters have already been enslaved, but it is not in our power to help it" (5:5). Nehemiah "very angry"; nobles swear restitution. Angle: "respectable" bondage by fellow believers; what levers does a church have against exploitative lenders?
- **Esther 2.** Virgins "gathered," prepared a year, sent to the king one by one — state-run sexual acquisition; no consent. Shiloh Project "#UsToo" essay: https://shilohproject.blog/for-such-a-time-as-this-ustoo-sexual-trafficking-silence-secrecy-in-the-book-of-esther/ . NB: the Safe House Project study's Lesson 8 is titled "For Such A Time As This" — ask whether 4:14 sanctifies or redeems what happened to her.
- **Daniel 1.** Youths deported, renamed, trained for a foreign court — forced relocation and identity erasure are standard trafficking tactics. Angle: what preserves personhood under coercion?
- **Lamentations 5:11-13.** "Women are raped in Zion... Young men are compelled to grind at the mill, and boys stagger under loads of wood." Use as congregational lament.
- **Hosea and Gomer (Hos 1-3).** "So I bought her for fifteen shekels of silver and a homer and a lethech of barley" (3:2). Redemption-by-purchase. CAUTION: metaphor can be heard as blaming the exploited woman.
- **Ezekiel 16.** 16:4-7 — the Bible's most tender picture of God adopting a discarded newborn; the rest of the chapter is severe. Read 16:1-14 with survivors in mind; discuss honestly why the rest is hard.
- **Onesimus (Philemon).** "no longer as a bondservant but more than a bondservant, as a beloved brother" (v. 16); "you will do even more than I say" (v. 21). Why persuade rather than command? (McKnight, McCaulley below.)
- **Rahab (Josh 2; 6; Heb 11:31; Jas 2:25; Matt 1:5).** Scripture refuses to define a woman by the sex trade; honored for faith.
- **Women Jesus encountered.** Luke 7:36-50; John 4; John 8:1-11; Matt 21:31 ("the tax collectors and the prostitutes go into the kingdom of God before you"). Angle: John 8 — where is the man? What does it say to a culture that arrests sellers but not buyers?

### A2. Laws that directly prohibit trafficking
- **Exod 21:16 (ESV):** "Whoever steals a man and sells him, and anyone found in possession of him, shall be put to death." **Deut 24:7:** "If a man is found stealing one of his brothers... and if he treats him as a slave or sells him, then that thief shall die." Same penalty as murder; Exod 21:16 covers the BUYER/possessor — a biblical demand-side law. Many interpreters read the eighth commandment as primarily about man-stealing.
- **Deut 23:15-16:** "You shall not give up to his master a slave who has escaped... He shall dwell with you... You shall not wrong him." Nearly unique in the ANE (Hammurabi punished harboring runaways). Angle: a "sanctuary church"?
- **Exod 21:2-11, 20-21, 26-27; Deut 15:12-18; Lev 25.** Six-year limit, mandatory release with provision ("furnish him liberally," Deut 15:14); injury → freedom; Jubilee; kin redemption. The law builds exit ramps. Angle: modern exit ramps (housing, legal status, debt forgiveness) — who funds them?
- **Lev 19:29:** "Do not profane your daughter by making her a prostitute, lest the land fall into prostitution." **Deut 23:17-18** bans cult prostitution and its earnings. Familial sex trafficking named; guilt located on the parent.
- **Lev 19:33-34.** Sojourner treated as native, "for you were strangers in Egypt." Migrants/foreign workers are the most trafficked population — Maryland agriculture, hospitality, domestic work.

### A3. Prophetic denunciations of selling people
- **Amos 2:6-7:** "they sell the righteous for silver, and the needy for a pair of sandals." **Amos 8:4-6:** "that we may buy the poor for silver and the needy for a pair of sandals." Commodification as the defining sin of a prosperous, religious nation. Angle: what is a person "worth" in the illicit economy?
- **Joel 3:3:** "have cast lots for my people, and have traded a boy for a prostitute, and have sold a girl for wine and have drunk it"; 3:6 sold Judah's children "to the Greeks." The most explicit biblical picture of child sex trafficking. God: "return your payment on your own head" (3:4,7).
- **Ezek 27:13:** Tyre's partners "exchanged human beings and vessels of bronze for your merchandise." People next to bronze on a cargo manifest. Pair with Rev 18:13; forced-labor goods in our shopping carts.
- **Nahum 3:4.** ESV "betrays nations with her whorings"; NIV "enslaved nations by her prostitution"; Hebrew *mākar* "to sell" (KJV "selleth nations"). Empires profiting from exploitation judged as pimps.
- **Jer 34:8-22.** Owners "took back the male and female slaves" (34:11); "I proclaim to you liberty to the sword, to pestilence, and to famine" (34:17). Re-enslaving the freed = covenant-breaking. Angle: re-trafficking (relapse, debt, deportation) and prevention.
- **Isa 58:6:** "to loose the bonds of wickedness... to let the oppressed go free, and to break every yoke." **Isa 1:17; 10:1-2; 61:1-3; Jer 22:3; Zech 7:9-10; Mic 6:8.** Isaiah 58 makes liberation a form of worship.

### A4. New Testament
- **Luke 4:18-19:** "to proclaim liberty to the captives... to set at liberty those who are oppressed." Venter & Semmelink church-response model: https://www.gahts.com/publications/ecjl9xjsxz95fhg-gns5a-n72na
- **Matt 18:6-10; Matt 25:31-46; Luke 10:25-37.** The Samaritan's two denarii and promise to return = long-term survivor care, not one-time rescue.
- **1 Tim 1:9-10.** ἀνδραποδισταῖς (*andrapodistais*; sg. *andrapodistēs*) — "enslavers" (ESV), "slave traders" (NIV/NASB), "menstealers" (KJV). From ἀνδράποδον "man-footed [creature]," slave slang on the pattern of τετράποδον (livestock). Thayer: "a slave-dealer, kidnapper, man-stealer." Only NT occurrence; the list tracks the Decalogue so it stands where "You shall not steal" falls. Paul puts slave trading beside murder and perjury as "contrary to sound doctrine." Lexicon: https://biblehub.com/lexicon/1_timothy/1-10.htm ; https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/andrapodistes
- **Rev 18:11-13.** "slaves, that is, human souls" (ESV); "human beings sold as slaves" (NIV). Greek σωμάτων καὶ ψυχὰς ἀνθρώπων — σῶμα "body" was merchants' jargon for a slave; John adds "souls" to strip the euphemism. The merchants weep for lost profit, not the enslaved. Where is our economy "Babylon"?
- **Gal 3:28; 1 Cor 6:15-20; 1 Cor 7:23** ("You were bought with a price; do not become bondservants of men"); **Eph 6:9; James 5:1-6** (withheld wages "cry out"). If every body is bought by Christ, what is the status of a second, human purchase?

### A6. Handling the Bible's own slavery texts honestly
Hard texts: Exod 21:2-11, 20-21; Lev 25:44-46; Eph 6:5; Col 3:22; 1 Pet 2:18.
- Christopher J. H. Wright, *Old Testament Ethics for the People of God* (IVP 2004): Israel's institution "qualitatively vastly different"; *'ebed* ranges from dependent to royal official; debt-driven, time-limited, protected.
- Paul Copan, *Is God a Moral Monster?* (Baker 2011) chs 12-14: "indentured servanthood"; kidnapping-for-sale capital; runaways sheltered. https://chab123.wordpress.com/2012/02/09/why-is-the-new-testament-silent-on-slavery-or-is-it-by-paul-copan/
- Peter J. Williams (Tyndale House), "Does the Bible Support Slavery?" lecture 2015: https://www.laniertheologicallibrary.org/messages/does-the-bible-support-slavery/ ; summary https://www.bethinking.org/bible/does-the-bible-support-slavery
- Esau McCaulley, *Reading While Black* (IVP 2020) ch 7: Philemon, 1 Cor 7:21-24, 1 Tim 1:10 undercut slavery from within; Black church's Exodus reading.
- Scot McKnight, *The Letter to Philemon* (NICNT, Eerdmans 2017): Paul asks for manumission.
- William J. Webb, *Slaves, Women and Homosexuals* (IVP 2001): redemptive-movement hermeneutic.
Honest caveats: (1) Lev 25:44-46 does permit permanent ownership of foreigners; Exod 21:20-21 treats a servant's death differently — "indentured" framing fits debt-servitude better than these verses. (2) NT never commands abolition; it plants seeds later harvested. (3) Decisive for this topic: the Bible NEVER permits kidnapping, selling, or sexually exploiting a person for profit — it punishes it with death — so trafficking is condemned by every strand even where servitude is regulated.

### Suggested use
12-week track with *God of Justice* as spine; supplement with narratives (Joseph, Exodus, 2 Kgs 4-5, Neh 5, Esther, Philemon), one law/prophets week (Exod 21:16, Deut 23:15-16, Amos, Joel 3), one NT word-study week (1 Tim 1:10, Rev 18:13), one "hard texts" week (Williams/Copan), a film night (*I Am Jane Doe*), and a final week with Araminta or FAAST to turn study into local action.
