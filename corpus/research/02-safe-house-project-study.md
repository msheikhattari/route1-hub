## 2. OnWatch (https://www.iamonwatch.org/)

- **Built by:** Malouf Foundation (Utah) with SHP, developed with survivors. Malouf's first version 2018; joint platform launched Sept 24, 2020. Mission: "unifies the voices of survivors and expertise of industry leaders to equip communities to combat sex trafficking in the United States."
- **Format:** 10 modules, ~1 hour, free, progress auto-saves (https://www.iamonwatch.org/training/overview). Survivors tell their own stories — Julie Whitehead; Alia Dewees (trafficked at 14 by an older "boyfriend"; schools module, Sept 2021); "Hope" (trafficked ages 6-11). 30-min educator version (https://www.iamonwatch.org/training/signup#educator). Not for viewing with children.
- **Signs taught** (Malouf's Jake Neeley, July 2024): signs of physical abuse or malnourishment; dressed inappropriately for age or weather; controlling parent/guardian/partner; avoids eye contact and interaction; rehearsed or forced answers. Schools module adds emotional outbursts, swings between disengaged and thriving, daytime effects of nighttime abuse. Core message: trafficking "is not typically someone locked up in a basement" — it is grooming and manipulation, often by someone known. Homepage stats: average first-trafficking age 12; 40% sold by family members; 93% increase in online enticement reports during COVID. (Treat as advocacy estimates.)
- **Reporting taught:** National Human Trafficking Hotline 1-888-373-7888 / text "Info" to 233733. SHP separately runs 1-833-5-BESAFE and the Simply Report app (https://www.simplyreport.com/).
- **Certificate/stats:** No individual completion certificate documented. SHP claims 510,000+ trained overall (550,000+ as of July 2026).
- **Group version:** "OnWatch Advocate" — a church/business/school registers as a group (https://www.iamonwatch.org/groups; optional Group Code at signup); at 90% completion it receives a one-year certification badge and marketing materials.

## 3. Safe House Project — the organization

- **Founders:** Kristi Wells (CEO) and Brittany Dunn (COO; appointed to Virginia's Governor's Commission on Human Trafficking Prevention), founded 2017 in Hampton Roads, VA. 501(c)(3) May 2018, EIN 82-3487081.
- **HQ:** 1340 N. Great Neck Rd, Ste 1272-162, Virginia Beach, VA 23454 (ProPublica/Charity Navigator list Alexandria, VA).
- **Mission:** "eradicate child trafficking in the U.S. by increasing survivor identification, providing emergency services, and ensuring access to safe housing and holistic care." 2021 stated goal: eradicate child sex trafficking in America by 2030.
- **Programs:** (1) training — OnWatch, H.O.P.E. healthcare CE, Just Ask Prevention youth curriculum, In-Home Service Training, CORE 13-session staff training, Ally Training, God's Heart; (2) emergency response — Response Line 1-833-5-BESAFE, 7am-7pm ET daily, partly volunteer "Response Line Navigators"; Simply Report app (May 2025; 22,900 tips in 2025); (3) Safe Housing Network — 4,300+ vetted resources, 1,100+ residential programs, 624 bed-spaces funded; (4) Safe House Certification — Gold/Silver/Bronze/Emerging Leader tiers; (5) Policy — Trafficking Survivor Equity Coalition; (6) Research — annual impact reports, 2026 "Patterns of Exploitation" report (https://www.safehouseproject.org/resources/).
- **Scale/finances:** 6,800-7,000+ survivors served since 2020 (534 in 2023, 1,529 in 2024, 2,644 in 2025). 2024 revenue $3.74M, expenses $2.39M. Charity Navigator 4/4 stars, 97% (2024). No ECFA accreditation found.
- **Leadership/board:** VP Programs Kathleen Arnold, MSW; Director of Collective Impact Alia Azariah (survivor); board chair John Allen Waldrop, Esq.; vice chair Scott Hasenbalg (IJM).
- **Maryland connections:** SHP co-leads the Frederick County Enhanced Collaborative Model anti-trafficking task force with State's Attorney Charlie Smith, $1.06M 3-year DOJ OVC grant (Nov 2024). No SHP-certified safe home in Maryland identified. Don't confuse with Baltimore's unrelated "Safe House of Hope."
- **Critiques/cautions:** (a) headline stats ("300,000 children trafficked annually," "1% identified," "80% re-trafficked") are advocacy estimates researchers broadly dispute; (b) Feb 2025 SHP said the Polaris-run National Hotline "has lost all credibility" and urged RFK Jr.'s confirmation to reform it; Polaris disputes the framing; (c) partnerships with Flock Safety and an AI reporting app raise privacy questions; (d) a Sept 2025 WV News story about a Montana safe house "spiritual abuse" allegation appears on SHP's press page but now 404s — involvement unconfirmed.

## 5. Running Lesson 2 ("Confronting the Darkness – The Realities of Trafficking")
Dunn's talk likely tracks SHP's standard "realities" points (OnWatch FAQ https://www.iamonwatch.org/faq): TVPA definition (force, fraud, coercion — or any minor in commercial sex); traffickers usually known (boyfriends, family, peers); recruitment is grooming, not kidnapping; why victims stay (trauma bonds, shame, dependency); trafficking vs. smuggling; how viral misinformation harms identification.

Suggested questions, Part 1 (Oct 15): What did you believe trafficking looked like before tonight, and where did that picture come from? Why pair "darkness" with Matthew 16's "gates of Hades" — what does it mean that Jesus declared victory at a place of exploitation? Which vulnerabilities named (foster care, runaways, online enticement, family instability) exist in Howard County? Who in our congregation already sees at-risk kids (youth leaders, teachers, nurses, coaches)?

Part 2 (Oct 29, after OnWatch): Which survivor story stayed with you? Which of the five signs would you have missed a month ago? Noticing vs. investigating — why does OnWatch insist you not confront? How would our church respond if a teenager disclosed? Who do we call (1-888-373-7888 / 1-833-5-BESAFE / 911)? Close: the realities are dark but the frame is revival — where is God already at work locally?

Cautions: treat statistics as estimates; budget time after OnWatch since survivor content can surface disclosures in the room; L3-8 move from realities to healing, authority, mobilization, so L2 should end with "now that we see, what will we do?"
