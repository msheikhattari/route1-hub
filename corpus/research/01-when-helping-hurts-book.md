## 1. Chapter-by-chapter

### Oct 1 — Opening Exercise, Introduction, Ch 1 (pp. 19–47)
**Opening Exercise (p. 19):** Indonesia 2004 tsunami; your church sends a team four months later to help restart small businesses. Groups answer: how will you plan/prepare; what resources; whom to send; what the team does; components of ministry; how to implement. KEEP THE ANSWERS — revisited in "Extended Exercise: Indonesia Reconsidered" (pp. 143–148, Dec 3).

**Introduction (p. 21):** Two convictions — N. American Christians aren't doing enough about poverty, and the methods used "often do considerable harm to both the materially poor and the materially non-poor." Thesis: "Until we embrace our mutual brokenness, our work with low-income people is likely to do far more harm than good."

**Part 1 Initial Thoughts:** 1. Why did Jesus come to earth? 2. For what specific sin(s) was OT Israel sent into captivity? (be specific) 3. What is the primary task of the church?

**Ch 1: Why Did Jesus Come to Earth? (pp. 31–47)**
- Luke 4:17–21, 4:43: Jesus' mission is the kingdom — "I am using My power to fix everything that sin has ruined" (p. 32). Kingdom "now" and "not yet."
- Survey: most evangelicals say "Jesus came to die... so we can go to heaven" — true but Col 1:15–20 makes him "Creator, Sustainer, and Reconciler of everything" (p. 33).
- Word and deed: Luke 7:22–23; blind beggar thought experiment (p. 35).
- Rev. Marsh (Laurel, MS) vs. KKK's Sam Bowers: "Reverend Marsh sought the King without the kingdom. The civil rights workers sought the kingdom without the King" (p. 37).
- Israel as "sneak preview": Sabbath/Jubilee/gleaning; Deut 15:4; Isa 1 & 58 — exile amid vigorous religiosity for failing the poor (pp. 38–39). Acts 4:34.
- Task of the church: embody Jesus "using both words and deeds... among the blind, the lame, the sick and outcast, and the poor" (p. 41). Rodney Stark on early church; Emperor Julian.
- "The Great Reversal" (1900–1930) evangelical retreat; "important but not exclusive task"; Rwanda 1994 as failure of all-of-life kingdom worldview (pp. 43–46).
- Reflection Qs: (1) your answer to why Jesus came and how it shapes your life; (2) did you know Israel was exiled partly for failing the poor — what does the church's ignorance say about how it reads Scripture; (3) your church's implicit answer to the church's primary task; (4) when poor people look at your church, do they see Jesus' comprehensive healing; (5) list three specific things you will do.

### Oct 15 — Ch 2: What's the Problem? (pp. 49–68)
**Initial Thoughts:** "What is poverty? Make a list of words that come to your mind."
**Argument:** "the true poverty experts, the poor themselves" (p. 49). "Poor people typically talk in terms of shame, inferiority, powerlessness, humiliation, fear, hopelessness, depression, social isolation, and voicelessness. North American audiences tend to emphasize a lack of material things" (p. 50). Via Bryant Myers (*Walking with the Poor*): **four foundational relationships** — God, self, others, rest of creation (Fig 2.1, p. 54); the Fall broke all four → **poverty of spiritual intimacy, poverty of being, poverty of community, poverty of stewardship**. Myers: "Poverty is the result of relationships that do not work... Poverty is the absence of shalom." "We are all broken, just in different ways" (p. 61). Jayakumar Christian: the rich carry **"god-complexes"** — "a subtle and unconscious sense of superiority... anointed to decide what is best for low-income people." Fikkert's confession (Rom 7:21). Parable of "Creekside Community Church" Christmas toys — fathers slip out the back door in shame; church slides into contempt and compassion fatigue. **The equation (p. 64):** Material definition of poverty + God-complexes of materially non-poor + Feelings of inferiority of materially poor = Harm to both. Way out: relational definition, repentance ("I am not okay; and you are not okay; but Jesus can fix us both"), treat the poor as people with gifts — "most importantly our ears."
**Reflection Qs (10):** 1 your four relationships; 2 Rom 5:6–11 — worthy of God's love?; 3 your god-complex and steps to change; 4 what really motivates you to help the poor; 5 evidence of god-complex in your church; 6 a past ministry — did it heal or deepen the four poverties for both sides; 7 same for your church; 8 compare your "what is poverty" list to the poor's own words; 9 do you hold a material definition — what harm; 10 are you locked into the p. 64 equation.
→ For the trafficking pairing, linger on Q3, Q4, Q9.

### Oct 29 — Ch 3: Are We There Yet? (pp. 71–95)
Poverty alleviation = "the ministry of reconciliation: moving people closer to glorifying God by living in right relationship with God, with self, with others, and with the rest of creation" (p. 74). Material poverty alleviation = people supporting themselves through their own work; "work is an act of worship." Goal ≠ middle-class Americans. Causes both individual and systemic ("liberals are right, conservatives are right, both incomplete") plus demonic; the "spider's web." "People and processes, not projects and products." Sandtown, Baltimore (Gornik/Tibbels) story — LOCAL HOOK. "Ultimately... cannot be done without people accepting Jesus Christ as Lord and Savior." Reflection: King Asa (2 Chr 16; Ps 20:7); ask materially poor/minority neighbors how local systems are unjust.

### Nov 5 — Ch 4: Not All Poverty Is Created Equal (pp. 99–116)
**Relief** (urgent temporary aid; one-way; when people truly can't help themselves), **rehabilitation** (restore pre-crisis; participation begins), **development** (ongoing change toward right relationships). "One of the biggest mistakes... by far—is applying relief in situations in which rehabilitation or development is the appropriate intervention." "Avoid paternalism. Do not do things for people that they can do for themselves" — five forms: **resource, spiritual, knowledge, labor, managerial**. Uganda penicillin story. Reflection: map every poverty-related org in your community by relief/rehab/development → do this for anti-trafficking orgs.

### Nov 12 — Ch 5: Give Me Your Tired, Your Poor, and Their Assets (pp. 119–131)
**Asset-based community development (ABCD)** over needs-based. "We are not bringing Christ to poor communities. He has been active in these communities since the creation of the world" (p. 122). Four elements: mobilize local capabilities; solutions from within; rebuild local relationships; outside resources only when local insufficient. Tools: asset mapping, Participatory Learning and Action, Appreciative Inquiry. Phil 4:8, 1 Thess 5:11.

### Dec 3 — Ch 6: McDevelopment (pp. 133–148)
Colombian rice co-op's rusting donated equipment; $2.3T in aid. Culprit: inadequate participation. **Blueprint approach** vs **learning process approach**. "Participation is not just the means to an end but a legitimate end in its own right." Table 6.1 levels of participation. Extended Exercise: Indonesia Reconsidered (pp. 143–148) + "The Rest of the Story."

### Dec 10 — Part 3, Ch 7–9 (pp. 151–201)
Ch 7 STMs: 120k (1989) → 2.2M (2006), ~$1.6B/yr; "elephant dancing with a mouse"; eight fixes. Ch 8 Yes, in Your Backyard: more poor in suburbs than cities (p. 169) — HOWARD COUNTY HOOK; jobs preparedness, financial education, wealth accumulation (IDAs); survey payday lenders/pawnshops. Ch 9: microfinance vs savings & credit associations; Promotion / Partnership / Complementary Training; Business as Mission.

### Jan 7 — Ch 10: Excuse Me, Can You Spare Some Change? (pp. 205–221)
Five principles of change: triggers; supportive community ("insufficient number of people willing to... walk through time with a needy individual or family"); early recognizable success ("start small, start soon, and succeed"); learn context as you go; start with the most receptive. → Essentially a survivor-mentoring model.

### Jan 28 — Ch 11: On Your Mark, Get Set, Go! + A Final Word (pp. 223–249)
"Parkview Church" case; Fig 11.1 three contexts. Inventory church gifts; benevolence policies; partner don't clone (John 17:20–23; Phil 2:1–11); the "Donald Trump Effect" — a N. American is a tycoon in a poor community so suggestions become directives; don't help households at the community's expense. Calls for *increased* giving to gospel-focused, asset-based, participatory ministries. Final Word: the most important step is personal repentance.

## 3. Applying the frameworks to anti-trafficking
Neither the book nor Chalmers has a dedicated trafficking resource (one Chalmers story: Branch15, OK — https://chalmers.org/blog/stories-of-faithfulness-learning-the-value-of-wholeness-and-sustainability-through-innovate/ ; orphan-care piece on how outside money makes "children... commodities": https://chalmers.org/resources/blog/helping-orphans-and-vulnerable-children-without-hurting/). But survivor-led anti-trafficking literature independently reaches the book's conclusions:
- **God-complex / "rescue" narrative.** ACF Region 4 *Guiding Principles*: "Using the word 'rescue'... places the service provider in the power-heavy role of 'rescuer'"; all interventions should be empowering and not replicate control (https://ncct.gsu.edu/files/2022/07/Guiding-Principles-for-Agencies-16-FINAL.pdf). Polaris survivors: "Words like 'rescue' turn people off"; "I had always had a voice" (https://polarisproject.org/listen-to-survivors/). The Exodus Road retired "Search + Rescue" (https://theexodusroad.com/should-we-use-the-word-rescue/). Love146 does not "rescue." "Arrest Is Not Rescue" (https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/arrest_is_not_rescue/). → Ch 2's equation in another key.
- **Poverty of being / "doing for."** New Friends New Life: savior posture tells women "they aren't enough" (https://www.newfriendsnewlife.org/trafficking-tuesdays/2022/2/17/survivors-of-human-trafficking-you-arent-their-savior).
- **Knowledge paternalism / partnership.** Survivor "Ava": educate yourselves, partner with experts, drop Christianese, say "prostituted woman," don't assume prayer replaces therapy (https://religionunplugged.com/news/2021/6/17/a-survivor-debunks-christian-myths-about-sex-trafficking). Nazarene Compassionate Ministries church guide: respect self-determination; beware gravitating to "the grandest of actions (opening a shelter...)" which "may come from a place of pride (Phil 2:3)"; never do street outreach untrained (https://ncm.org/sites/default/files/2021-03/ncm_anti-trafficking-church-engagement_us-focus.pdf).
- **Relief → rehab → development** maps onto emergency shelter → trauma therapy → economic empowerment. Raleigh Sadler *Vulnerable* (B&H 2019): "theology of glory" (dramatic rescue) vs "theology of the cross" (long recovery); churches beside foster youth/immigrants/homeless already do prevention+intervention+aftercare (https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/reviews/human-trafficking-victims-church/).
- **Spiritual paternalism — sharpest warning.** Knight et al. 2026, *Religions*, "I Created a God That Was Like a Good Parent" (https://doi.org/10.3390/rel17030348): survivors report from faith-based providers "retraumatizing judgments... spiritual bypassing... coercive practices," help conditioned on identity — compared by some to trafficking itself. Psychology Today Aug 2026 "What Happens After the Rescue?" — 63% of US trafficking shelters are Christian-affiliated (https://www.psychologytoday.com/nz/blog/counseling-keys/202608/what-happens-after-the-rescue).
Bridge questions: Where does our curriculum use "rescue" language and whom does it center? Which stage does each local partner do — are we funding the dramatic stage and starving the slow one? What assets do survivors already have, and do survivor-led orgs sit at our table?

## 4. Critiques to read with discernment
- Paralysis/misuse — Corbett: "When we hear stories of people using the book to do less, it is really hurtful" (https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/helping-hurts-turns-10-one-book-changed-care-poor/). Musekura: replace "helping" with "mutually giving."
- Theological right — 9Marks: under-weights evangelism, misreads exile, conflates Christ's mission with church's (https://www.9marks.org/review/when-helping-hurts/).
- Practitioners — Monty Lynn (*Missio Dei*): lacks case studies/macro policy (https://missiodeijournal.com/issues/md-2-1/authors/md-2-1-lynn); Susan Baker (*JUM*): too little on systemic oppression (https://jofum.com/reviews/review-when-helping-hurts/).
- Left/structural — Goodreads critics: "ghetto worldview," locating causes in self-discipline, aid-needs-evangelism claim, microfinance over-reliance; "substitutes one paternalism for another."
- Authors' own revision — *Becoming Whole* (2019): "Why should we think the goal... is to turn the poor into us when we know that we're not flourishing?"
**For the trafficking pairing weigh most:** (a) structural — trafficking is driven by demand, housing insecurity, foster-care pipelines, immigration law, which the relational lens treats lightly; (b) evangelism-conditionality — help tied to religious participation is experienced as coercion. Ch 2's own tools (god-complex, poverty of being, "our ears") are the best internal corrective.
