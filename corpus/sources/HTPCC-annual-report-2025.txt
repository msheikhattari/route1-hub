OVERVIEW and BACKGROUND
In 2014, Howard County commissioned its first Human Trafficking
Task Force, which recommended expanding services for victims,
increasing community-wide awareness and education, and
strengthening coordination among county agencies and community-
based organizations. In response, the Office of Human Trafficking
Prevention (OHTP) was established within the Department
of Community Resources and Services (DCRS) in 2017, along
with an Executive Order creating the Howard County Human
Trafficking Prevention Coordination Council (HTPCC). The council
was charged with reviewing and prioritizing the 2014 Task Force
recommendations and advising the county executive on sustainable
structures to coordinate and strengthen anti-trafficking efforts.

The HTPCC began meeting in mid-2017 and was made permanent
through county legislation in 2019 (CB 52-2019). Later that
year, the HTPCC and DCRS issued a multi-year report outlining
recommendations to guide future prevention and response efforts.

The HTPCC is a multidisciplinary body of residents, professionals
and service providers dedicated to advancing awareness, education,
survivor support, and cross-sector collaboration in partnership
with OHTP. The council convenes regular full-council meetings and
bi-monthly subcommittee meetings, working closely with partners
across healthcare, homeless services and organizations serving
foreign-born communities to strengthen prevention and response
efforts throughout Howard County.

Data and Trends in Howard County
Data collected in 2025 from law enforcement and direct service providers reflect the continued
presence of human trafficking in Howard County and highlight the importance of coordinated
prevention, identification, and response efforts across systems. While each data source represents
a different point of entry — law enforcement intervention and voluntary service engagement —
together they provide a more comprehensive understanding of trafficking trends, locations, and
service needs within the county.


Law Enforcement Trends
Based on quarterly reporting from the Howard County Police Department (HCPD), 35 new victims
of sex trafficking were recovered and offered services during calendar year 2025. These cases were
identified across hotel-based, residential and illicit massage business (IMB) settings, with IMB-related
investigations increasing in the latter part of the year.


Direct Service Provider Trends
Service-provider data further illustrates the scope and complexity of trafficking-related needs
in the county. In 2025, direct service providers served 48 new individuals, including survivors of
trafficking and individuals identified as being at high risk. The majority experienced sex trafficking,
with labor trafficking and combined forms also identified. A significant portion were classified as
high risk, underscoring the value of early identification and prevention-focused engagement before
exploitation escalates. Consistent with law enforcement trends, hotel-based, residential, and IMB-
related settings were the most commonly identified locations of exploitation.


Data Context
It is important to note that human trafficking remains significantly underreported, and available
data reflect only known and identified cases. Differences in reporting timelines, survivor choice,
and system access influence how data are captured across agencies. An increase in identification
numbers does not necessarily indicate that more trafficking is occurring in Howard County; rather, it
often reflects improved training, stronger cross-sector coordination, enhanced investigative capacity,
and increased community awareness. In this context, a rise in identified cases can represent
progress in recognizing and responding to exploitation. Continued investment in training, outreach
and collaborative systems is essential to ensure that individuals experiencing trafficking are
identified earlier and connected to appropriate services.

ACTIVITIES, HIGHLIGHTS AND TRAININGS
Subcommittees
In 2025, the HTPCC successfully reestablished its subcommittee structure. All subcommittees were
formally established and began meeting regularly throughout the latter part of the year. These
subcommittees were designed to support focused, action-oriented work aligned with the council’s
mandate, while allowing members to engage deeply in specific priority areas.


CORE Team Subcommittee
The CORE Team Subcommittee serves as a collaborative forum for direct service providers and
system partners to coordinate around human trafficking trends, service gaps, and system-level
challenges in Howard County. Originally established through the Office for Victims of Crime (OVC)
Enhanced Collaborative Model (ECM), the CORE Team provides structured space for case-informed
discussion while maintaining strict confidentiality standards.
In 2025, the CORE Team met six times throughout the year, continuing its role as a multidisciplinary
body focused on coordinated case review and systems collaboration. Current member agencies
include: Howard County Police Department, TurnAround, Inc., HopeWorks, OHTP, and the Child
Advocacy Center. The subcommittee welcomed a new partner from the Department of Social
Services, Child Protective Services (CPS), strengthening cross-system coordination, particularly in
youth-related cases.
Throughout the year, members discussed emerging trafficking trends and identified gaps in services
impacting survivors and individuals at risk. The CORE Team also worked collaboratively to establish
and refine a countywide Data Collection Tool to improve reporting consistency, trend analysis, and
data-informed decision-making across agencies.
All participating members have signed confidentiality agreements to ensure the secure and ethical
handling of case information. The continued convening of this group strengthens interagency
communication, supports trauma-informed and survivor-centered practices, and advances a more
coordinated system of care for survivors in Howard County.


Legislative Subcommittee
The Legislative Subcommittee monitors and reviews local, state and federal policy developments
related to human trafficking, labor exploitation and victim protections. The subcommittee ensures
the council remains informed and responsive to legislative changes that impact prevention efforts
and service delivery.
Its goals include tracking relevant legislation, providing policy updates and recommendations to the
full council, identifying opportunities for recommendation and education, and supporting informed
strategic planning.


Training, Outreach and Prevention (TOP) Subcommittee
The TOP Subcommittee strengthens the capacity of professionals and community members
across Howard County to recognize, prevent and respond to human trafficking. The subcommittee
integrates training, outreach and prevention strategies by identifying priority audiences,
coordinating trauma-informed and survivor-informed education, and promoting consistent
prevention messaging across sectors.

      In 2025, the TOP Subcommittee supported several targeted training initiatives across key systems.
      These efforts included training more than 300 Student Support Staff within the Howard County
      Public School System (HCPSS), strengthening school-based awareness of trafficking indicators,
      student vulnerabilities, and appropriate reporting and referral pathways. The subcommittee
      also supported human trafficking training for leadership staff at the Howard County Department
      of Corrections, increasing awareness of trafficking dynamics, victim identification, and trauma-
      informed response within the correctional setting. In addition, training was delivered to public
      health nurses, enhancing their ability to recognize indicators of trafficking in healthcare settings and
      connect individuals to appropriate services.
      Beyond professional training, the subcommittee advances community-based prevention efforts by
      engaging populations most vulnerable to exploitation, supporting culturally responsive outreach
      strategies, distributing educational resources, and strengthening cross-sector collaboration to
      address vulnerabilities before harm occurs.


      Screening and Assessment Tool Subcommittee
      The Screening and Assessment Tool Subcommittee was established to support the development of
      a standardized, countywide tool to improve the early identification of individuals experiencing or at
      risk of human trafficking. The subcommittee brings together cross-sector stakeholders to ensure
      the tool is trauma-informed, ethically grounded, and responsive to the realities of direct service
      providers.
      The subcommittee’s goals include promoting consistent identification and response practices across
      agencies, incorporating survivor-informed and provider-informed feedback, and strengthening
      responsible data collection and referral pathways. To support this work, members are actively
      reviewing existing screening tools and assessments used within the anti-trafficking field, with a focus
      on evidence-based and expert-recommended practices to inform local implementation.


      Human Trafficking Awareness and Prevention Month 2025
      In January 2025, in recognition of Human Trafficking Prevention Month, the Human Trafficking
      Prevention and Coordination Council (HTPCC) hosted a resource fair themed “Addressing the
      Vulnerabilities.” The event was designed to move beyond awareness and focus on the structural
      and individual vulnerabilities that increase risk for exploitation. Participating agencies were each
      assigned a specific vulnerability, such as housing instability, immigration status, economic insecurity,
      healthcare access, or system involvement, and attendees rotated between tables to learn how
      these factors impact a survivor’s ability to access resources and how agencies work to address these
      barriers.
      The event included participation from a range of Howard County agencies and community partners,
      including the Office of Human Rights and Equity, HopeWorks, Grassroots Crisis Intervention Center,
      Luminis, the Office of Human Trafficking Prevention, the Department of Community Resources and
      Services (DCRS), the Howard County Health Department (including HIV Prevention, Reproductive
      Health, Overdose Response, and Behavioral Health teams), Hon’s Honey, and Ayuda.
      The program also featured a Human Trafficking 101 presentation with a focused emphasis on
      labor trafficking, followed by a partial screening of Food Chains, which highlighted exploitation
      within agricultural labor systems and efforts to hold corporations accountable through worker
      empowerment. The screening was followed by an expert panel discussion examining labor
      trafficking in Howard County, populations most impacted, and strategies to strengthen survivor
      support. Collectively, the event reinforced the importance of coordinated, prevention-focused, and
      vulnerability-informed approaches to addressing human trafficking at the local level.

Ongoing Goals
These goals reﬂect the Council’s ongoing commitment to fostering a coordinated,
inclusive, and survivor-informed approach to preventing human trafﬁcking in
Howard County.

Centering Survivor Voices
Integrate survivor leadership and lived-experience perspectives into council initiatives, training
development and policy recommendations to ensure trauma-informed and survivor-informed
approaches.

Enhance Community Collaboration
Expand and deepen cross-sector partnerships with local agencies, council, commissions, and
neighboring jurisdictions to strengthen prevention and response efforts.

Advance Awareness for Vulnerabilities to Trafficking
Expand outreach efforts to meet the needs of populations most vulnerable to human trafficking,
using best-practice, victim-centered approaches that prioritize cultural responsiveness and equity.

Enhance Council Capacity
Work closely with county administration to fill vacant council appointments and ensure a fully
representative, engaged and effective membership body.

Events for Human Trafficking Prevention and Awareness Month
Begin work on planning a council-led event for January 2027.



New Goals for 2026
As the HTPCC reﬂects on the progress made throughout 2025, it remains committed
to advancing its original recommendations while setting forth new goals that will guide
its work in 2026. Looking ahead, the council will focus on the following priorities:

Strengthening Data Collection Tool
Refine and enhance the County’s Data Collection Tool to ensure ethical, consistent reporting and
improved analysis of trends and service gaps.

Create a County-wide Screening and Assessment Tool
Create and implement a standardized, trauma-informed screening tool to improve early
identification of trafficking victims and ensure consistent responses across agencies.

Expanding Social Media and Public Awareness Efforts
Strengthen digital outreach and public messaging to increase awareness of trafficking indicators and
available resources in Howard County.

Expand Policy Impact
Monitor and support implementation of state and federal legislation while identifying local policy
opportunities to reduce vulnerabilities and improve survivor protections.

     IN CONCLUSION
     In 2025, the Howard County Human Trafficking Prevention Coordination Council continued to
     build on prior outreach, training and collaborative efforts while placing increased emphasis on
     strengthening internal infrastructure, survivor-informed leadership, and coordinated prevention
     strategies. In partnership with the Office of Human Trafficking Prevention, the council supported a
     range of training and educational initiatives reaching school-based professionals, public health staff,
     law enforcement, corrections leadership, and other frontline systems across Howard County. These
     efforts reinforced awareness of trafficking indicators, prevention strategies, and response pathways
     across multiple sectors.

     Recognizing the importance of survivor leadership, the council advanced efforts to center lived
     experience within its work. In 2025, the appointment of a survivor leader to the HTPCC marked a
     significant milestone in strengthening survivor-informed decision-making and ethical practice across
     council activities. This progress reflects the council’s continued commitment to ensuring survivor
     perspectives shape prevention, outreach and policy discussions.

     Internally, the HTPCC made substantial progress in reestablishing its subcommittee structure
     and strengthening council capacity through new appointments and reappointments. Regular
     subcommittee convenings supported focused work on policy, training, outreach, data, and systems
     coordination, positioning the council for more targeted and measurable outcomes in the year
     ahead.

     Together, these efforts reflect the HTPCC’s ongoing dedication to fostering a coordinated, inclusive,
     and prevention-focused approach to human trafficking prevention in Howard County. As the council
     looks ahead to 2026, it remains committed to building on this foundation through survivor-informed
     leadership, strengthened partnerships, data-informed strategies, and sustained collaboration across
     county systems and the broader community.
