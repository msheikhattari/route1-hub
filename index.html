Audit hotel-training compliance in Howard County

Development

Maryland requires every lodging employee to be trained and every property to certify yearly, yet 82 of 765 did in 2025 and nobody publishes a county breakdown. Request the Department of Labor's certification list under the Public Information Act, map every hotel and motel on the Route 1, I-95 and BWI corridor, hand the gap list to the county council, then offer non-compliant properties the free approved trainings through the county's Allied Business Program. Nobody else is doing this.

Two or three people comfortable with spreadsheets · $0

Where the church stands, and a path forward

The church already has an outreach arm with a staff director and a budget line, a Route 1 prayer practice owned at the elder level, and a weekly presence with one of the most at-risk populations in the region. None of it is labeled anti-trafficking. All of it is.

A path forward

  1. October. Finish OnWatch as a registered church group. Book the county presentation. Loop overseer David Ross into the prayer drives.
  2. November. Council meeting Nov 19; offer the hotel audit. Start the Araminta drives. Decide who applies to HopeWorks on Dec 1.
  3. December. Delegation hearing Dec 21. Advent guide in front of the church. Dressember, or a parallel ask for a local partner.
  4. January. HopeWorks workshop at church. Wear Blue Day. Staff the county's awareness event. Draft the pilot's proposal to leadership.
  5. February. Put Araminta or HopeWorks on the Impact Weekend roster. Bakhita prayer night. Written testimony as bills are heard.
  6. Spring. Prevention education at The Children's Home. Apply for a faith seat or the Interfaith Advisory Commission. Hand the hotel gap list to the council.
  7. Summer. Report results to the church and launch a second group. Money moves to vetted Local Impact partners, so a project travels best as a partnership with HopeWorks or Araminta.

What Mosaic already brings

  • Impact, the church's outreach arm: Local Impact Director Natasha Ramcharran; ten percent of all giving; about $814,000 in 2025, mostly grants to partners.
  • Route 1 prayer, already coordinated by overseer David Ross; an August 2026 self-guided drive named seven Route 1 hotels as "hidden places of exploitation and trafficking."
  • The Children's Home in Catonsville: a weekly Saturday flag-football group with teenagers placed there after abuse or neglect.
  • Celebrate Recovery on Thursdays, Sexual Integrity 101 and divorce support: the vulnerabilities traffickers use.
  • Partners today: Mount Clare Christian School, 10:12 Sports, Wellspring Life Ministry's pregnancy clinics, Operation Christmas Child, eleven church plants, five churches abroad. Mosaic's resource list already names Grassroots and HopeWorks.
  • Not yet connected: Araminta, TurnAround, the county office, Catherine's Cottage, FAAST, the SAFE Center.
  • Ask staff: an older public description says "for every first-time guest, we give money to fight sex trafficking." If it still exists, where does it go?
